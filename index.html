The lab thesis
Mental health is not one data type. Neither should its AI be.

Our work connects human interaction, population evidence, and brain signals to build computational systems that are useful in the messy reality of mental health care—not only on benchmarks.

Research programs

Three programs. One problem.

We organize the lab around three connected questions: how AI can work with clinicians and patients, how those systems should be evaluated, and how biological signals can support more precise intervention.

01

Human-governed clinical AI

Agentic and generative AI for psychiatric interviewing, psychotherapy training, clinical simulation, and decision support—with humans kept inside the loop.

Core question: How should autonomous systems participate in care without pretending to replace clinical judgment?
Explore program →
02

Safety, evidence & causal learning

Stress-testing foundation models, validating digital twins, studying guardrails and sycophancy, and using interpretable and causal ML to understand mental-health outcomes.

Core question: What evidence is enough before an AI system deserves trust in a high-stakes setting?
Explore program →
03

NeuroAI & precision neuromodulation

Computational approaches to EEG, neural biomarkers, brain stimulation, neural coding, and individualized neurotechnology for neuropsychiatric disorders.

Core question: Can physiological signals help us move from population averages toward personalized intervention?

How we work

Build the system. Then try to break it.

Across projects, the methodological spine is the same: begin with a real clinical or scientific question, build the smallest system that can answer it, stress-test the assumptions, and measure what changes when humans and models interact.

01 · Ground

Start with the problem

Clinical workflow, patient need, population question, or physiological mechanism.

02 · Build

Construct the model

Agents, causal models, simulations, biomarkers, or signal-processing systems.

03 · Stress-test

Probe failure modes

Safety, fidelity, bias, calibration, robustness, and identity-specific validity.

04 · Govern

Keep humans in control

Human supervision, auditable decisions, and explicit boundaries on automation.

05 · Translate

Measure real use

Training, assessment, service design, personalized intervention, or reusable software.

Research / Program

Research across scales.

Our work connects computation to clinically meaningful questions: how intelligence should be evaluated, how mental-health heterogeneity can be understood, and how neural signals can guide more precise interventions.

Program logic

Not five isolated topics—a connected research system.

The same principles recur across the program: explicit assumptions, meaningful validation, interpretability where it matters, human oversight, and methods that can survive the transition from benchmark to real-world use.

01

Agentic & Generative AI for Psychiatry

We develop and evaluate agentic and generative AI systems for psychiatric assessment, clinical training, psychotherapy, and mental-health decision support. A central direction is moving beyond general-purpose chat toward structured, human-supervised systems that can be audited and evaluated against task-specific clinical objectives.

Multi-agent LLMsPsychiatric interviewingPsychotherapy fidelitySimulated patientsClinical workflow designHuman-in-the-loop AI
02

Responsible AI, Safety & Digital Twins

Capability is not the same as trustworthiness. We study how safety guardrails, model personas, sycophancy, toxicity detection, simulation fidelity, and digital-twin architectures influence model behaviour. The goal is empirical validation that asks whether an AI system is reliable, calibrated, and valid for its intended use—not whether it merely produces plausible outputs.

Safety guardrailsSycophancyPersona effectsToxicity detectionDigital twinsIdentity-specific validation
03

Population Mental Health & Causal Machine Learning

We combine epidemiologic reasoning, interpretable machine learning, and causal methods to understand heterogeneity in mental-health outcomes and access to care. Current questions include treatment-seeking, suicidality, depression, psychological distress, functional health, and how social and clinical information contributes to risk.

Causal MLInterpretable MLPsychiatric epidemiologyNational survey dataHealth servicesEquity
04

Computational Neurotechnology & Precision Neuromodulation

We bridge computational neuroscience with clinically relevant measurement and modulation of brain activity. Work includes neural signal processing, EEG automation, biomarker discovery, deep-brain-stimulation response, and emerging stimulation technologies with an emphasis on physiological signatures that can guide individualized intervention.

05

Neural Coding, Neurophysiology & Foundational Science

Foundational work examines how cellular, molecular, and biophysical mechanisms shape neural excitability, information transmission, and sensory coding. The program has studied membrane properties, ion-channel distributions, physiological noise, degeneracy, spike initiation, and multiplexed neural representation.

A translational principle

Validation should match the claim.

If a system claims to support clinical interviewing, evaluate the interview. If a digital twin claims to represent a person, test person-specific fidelity. If a biomarker claims to guide treatment, connect it to treatment-relevant outcomes. The unit of validation should be the unit of intended use.

What this looks like in practice

01 · FORMULATE
Define the use-case before the model.

Clinical or scientific need, intended users, assumptions, and failure modes.

02 · BUILD
Use the simplest architecture that can answer the question.

Agentic workflows, causal models, signal processing, or statistical learning as appropriate.

03 · STRESS-TEST
Evaluate behaviour under realistic variation.

Safety, bias, calibration, identity fidelity, or signal robustness.

04 · TRANSLATE
Design for human supervision and reproducibility.

Auditable outputs, transparent limitations, reusable code, and clinically interpretable endpoints.

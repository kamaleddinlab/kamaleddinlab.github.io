Research

From intelligent systems to evidence that matters.

Our work is organized by scientific questions—not technologies. We build systems, test where they fail, and connect computational outputs to decisions that clinicians and researchers can inspect.

01

Human-governed clinical AI

We design AI systems for tasks where conversational flexibility has to coexist with explicit clinical structure, bounded behavior, privacy, and human escalation.

  • Multi-agent structured psychiatric interviewing and screening
  • Simulated clinical encounters for psychiatry education
  • Psychotherapy fidelity assessment and targeted feedback
  • Human-supervised decision-support architectures
02

Trust, evaluation & causal evidence

A model can perform well on a benchmark and still fail in the interaction patterns that matter. We develop evaluation strategies that expose instability, guardrail effects, confounding, and clinically relevant failure modes.

  • Safety guardrails, sycophancy, and diagnostic instability
  • Stress tests for agentic and foundation models
  • Causal and interpretable machine learning
  • Population mental-health and treatment-seeking evidence
03

NeuroAI & precision neuromodulation

We use computational tools to make neurophysiology easier to analyze and brain stimulation more individualized—from EEG preprocessing to personalized models of non-invasive stimulation.

  • Human-in-the-loop EEG preprocessing and artifact removal
  • Neural biomarkers of treatment response
  • Personalized modeling of transcranial temporal interference stimulation
  • Computational neuroscience and neural coding
How we work

Methods are chosen around the question.

The lab spans several methodological traditions, but uses the same standard: transparent assumptions, reproducible workflows, and evaluation tied to intended use.

Current funded direction

Personalized non-invasive stimulation for depression.

Brain Canada supports ongoing work on personalized modeling and optimization of transcranial temporal interference stimulation (tTIS) in major depressive disorder, using individualized brain models and clinical trial data.

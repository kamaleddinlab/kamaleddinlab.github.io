Join / Collaborate

Bring a hard question, not just a model.

We welcome thoughtful inquiries from trainees, clinicians, researchers, and technical collaborators interested in rigorous work at the intersection of AI, mental health, causal evidence, and neurotechnology.

For trainees

You may be a good fit if…

  • You care about mental-health questions as much as model performance.
  • You want to understand why a method works, when it fails, and what assumptions it makes.
  • You are comfortable learning across disciplinary boundaries.
  • You want to produce reproducible analyses, software, experiments, or papers—not just demos.
For collaborators

We are especially interested in…

  • Clinical AI evaluation, simulated patients, and human–AI workflows.
  • Foundation-model safety, validation, and digital twins.
  • Causal and interpretable analysis of population mental-health data.
  • EEG, neural biomarkers, brain stimulation, and precision neuromodulation.
What working together looks like

Specific question. Shared ownership. Clear evidence.

Projects are strongest when the clinical or scientific context shapes the computational design from the beginning. We prefer a smaller, answerable question with a credible evaluation plan over a broad claim that cannot be tested.

01

Define the question

Clarify the decision, behavior, mechanism, or outcome that matters before choosing the method.

02

Build an evaluation plan

Decide what failure looks like, what comparison is meaningful, and what evidence would change our mind.

03

Make the work inspectable

Document assumptions, preserve reproducibility, and communicate the result clearly enough that others can challenge it.

Start a conversation

A short, specific email is the best first step.

For trainee inquiries, include your current program, a CV or résumé, and a few sentences about the research question or lab program that genuinely interests you. For collaboration inquiries, describe the problem, data or setting, and what you hope a computational partnership could add. Availability depends on current projects, supervision capacity, and funding.
